# Different Modals
## Modals
### Features
- No "s" in the third-person singular 
- Followed by a bare verb 
- Direct questions 
- Direct negation
- No -ing, -ed, or to forms 

### Common modals
- *Can*
- *Could*
- *Will*
- *Would*
- *Shall*
- *Should*
- *May*
- *Might*
- *Must*

## Semi-modals
### As verbs (common and informal)
- Normally used in affirmative sentences 
- Uses **"do/does"** 

  > *She **doesn't dare** to look*

- Takes **infinitive** when the **next word followed is a verb** 

  > *He **needs to** worry*

### As modals (formal)
- Normally used in **negative sentences** 
  - *Hardly*
  - *Never*
  - *Nobody*  

  > *The changes **need only** be small to make the proposals acceptable.*  


### Common semi-modals
- **Core** 
  - *Need*
  - *Dare*
  - *Used to*
  - *Ought to*

- **Phrasal**   
  They **act like normal** verb in grammar.  
  - *Have to/Have got to*
  - *Be able to*
  - *Be going to*
  - *Be supposed to*
  - *Had better*

# Asking Questions
## Ask for possibility 
### Could/Can/Be likely
- **Informal, daily settings** 

  >***Could** it be that you don't want to leave*

  >***Are** you **likely** to be in Spain again this summer*

### Might 
- **Formal**

  >***Might** they be persuaded to change their mind?*

## Ask for ability 
### Can 
- >***Can** you speak Russian?*

## Ask for permission 
### May
- Formally **asking for permission** and **offering help**
  
  > ***May** I help you?*

## Ask for necessity  
### Have 
- Hope for or expect a negative answer  

  >*Do we **have to** answer all the questions?*   

## Ask for advice/confirmation
### Should/Shall/Had better
- >***Should/Shall** I phone for a taxi for you*
  >***Had** we **better** get a taxi?*


# Ability
## General abilities 
### In action (can/could)
- **Can/could**  
  When talking about something that **is/was happening as we speak**
  - *The only thing/place/time*
  - *All (means the only thing)*

  >*Watch me, Mum; I **can** stand on one leg.*

  > ***All** we **could** see were is feet.*

  > *During the war, the police **could** arrest you for criticising the government.*

### Capability (can/could/be able to)
- **Can/could**  
  Meaning 'know how to'.  
  
  >*I **can** cook.*  

- **Be able to**  
  When confirming that you have the **time, physical means, or technical capacity to do something** in specific situations 
  
  >*I finished my homework early, so I **am able to** cook dinner tonight.*

### Senses or feelings (can/could)
- **Can/could**  

  **Sense verbs:** 
  - *Feel*
  - *Hear*
  - *Smell*
  - *Taste*

  **Stative verbs:** 
  - *Believe*
  - *Decide*
  - *Remember*
  - *Understand*
  
  > *I **could** remember the crash, but nothing after that.*

### Before passive (can/could/be able to)
- **Can/could**  

  >*Films **can** now easily **be streamed** online*

## Single past achievement (be able to)
- **Be able to**
  
  > *She swam strongly and **was able to** cross the river easily even though it was swollen by the heavy rain*  
  (A specific achievement)

## Something almost didn't happen (can/could)
- **Can/could**
  - *Almost*
  - *Hardly*
  - *Just*
  - *Nearly*
  
  > *I **could nearly** touch the ceiling.*

# Possibility 
## Definitely (can)  
- **Can**  
  It can be, and it **sometimes** is. Usually stating general facts.  
  
  >*It **can** be expensive to keep a cat.*
  
  > *We **can** stay with Jake in Oslo*

## Possible 
### Present events (may/might)  
- **May**  
  In **academic or formal language** to talk about characteristics or behaviours.     

  >*The seeds from the plant **may** grow up to 20 cm in length*

- **Might**  
  **Informal** speeches   

  >*I **might** paint the kitchen purple*

- **May/Might/Could + be + -ing**  
  An ongoing current event

  >*Marco isn't in his office. He **may/might/could be working** at home today*

### Past events (might/may/could have) 
- **May/Might/Could + have + (past participle)**  
  
  >*Do you think Laura **may/might have** completed the report by now?*

- **May/Might/Could + have been + -ing**  
  Past over a period of time.  

  >*Callum didn't know where the wall was, but he thought his sister **might/may/could have been playing** with it before she left for school.*

### Past typical events (might)
- **Might + (bare infinitive)**  
  Used to talk about what was **typically** the case in the **past** in **formal** or **literary** use

  > *During the war, the police **might** arrest you for criticising the government.*

### Future events (may/might/could)
- Use the expression for present and past event with a **future deadline** 
  >*His math **may/might/could have improved** **by the time** the exam comes round*

  >***When** I go to Vienna **I may/might/could be staying** with Max, but I'm not sure yet.*

## Likely to happen (should/ought to)
- **Should (common)** 
  
- **Ought to (formal)**

## Less likely to happen (could)
- **Could**  
  There is some doubt about

  > *We **could** stay with Jake in Oslo.*

## Something impossible (not)
- **Can't/Couldn't**  
  Theoretically or actually impossible
  
  > *There **can't** be many people in the world who haven't watched television*

## Theoretical possibility after something happened (could)
- **Could**  
  **If something happened**, it could, or it may not be.  

  >*It **could** be expensive to keep a cat.*

# Permission
## General action (can/could/be allowed to)
- **Can/could / Be allowed to**  
  
  > *Anyone **could/was allowed** to fish in the lake when the council owned it*

## Particular action (be allowed to)
- **Be allowed to**
  
  > *Although he didn't have a ticket, Ned **was allowed to** come in.*

## Negative sentences (not/needn't)
- **Couldn't/Can't/ Be not allowed**

  > *You **are not allowed** to enter!*

- **Needn't/don't need to**
  
  >*You **needn't cut** the grass, I'll do it later*  

# Facts & Occasion 
## Events
### General behaviour or habits (will/would)
- **Would/Will**  
  For past or present.  
  :warning: **NOT for particular occasions**

  > ***Every day** Dan **will** come home from work and turn on the TV*

  > ***Each time** I gave him a problem he **would** solve it for me*

- **Wouldn't/Won't**   
  Can be used for **either general or particular occasions** 

  >*He wouldn't walk the five miles to his place of work*

### Facts (will)
- **Will**
  Things that are **always true**
  
  >*Cold weather **will** kill certain plants*

  >*During the war, people **would** eat all kinds of things that we don't eat now.*

### Facts that are not necessarily true (needn't/don't have to)
- **Needn't/Don't have to**
  > *Volcanoes **needn't/don't have to** erupt constantly to be classified as active*

### Criticise in speech (will/would)
- We can **stress will or would** to **criticise** people's characteristic behaviour or habits.  

- Use **will** to express disapproval

  >*-I feel sick*  
  >*-If you **will** eat so much, I'm not surprised*

## Past finished events  
### Clear time reference (would/used to)
- **Would/Used to**  

  >*Whenever we went to my uncle's house, we **would/used to** play in the garden*

### Past states/Without clear time reference (used to)
- **Used to**

  >*We **used to** play in the garden*
  
  >*The factory **used to** be over there*

## Past situation 
### Past intention that didn't happen (would have)
- **Would have + (past participle)**  
  Imaginary situation or situation that **might have happened** in the past

  > *I **would have been happy** to see him, but I didn't have time*

### Assuming real (will have)
- **Will have + (past participle)**  
  Think a past situation **actually happened**.   

  >*As it was cloudy, few people **will have seen** last night's lunar eclipse*

# Obligation & Necessity 
## Formal rules and warnings (must/must not) 
- **Must/Must not**

  >*Bookings **must** be made at least seven days before departure*

## Based on evidence (must have to/must have had to)
- **Must have to**  
  Based on **current evidence** to make a **logical assumption** that someone else is forced or required to do something.  
  
  >*I can't access the database. You **must have to** put in a password.*

- **Must have had to**  
  Based on **past evidence**

  >*Matt wasn't at home when I went round. He must have had to go out unexpectedly*

## External obligation 
### Affirmative sentences (have (got) to/need to)
- **Have to (formal)**  
  When used with **frequency words.**
  - *Always*
  - *Never*
  - *Normally* 
  - *Rarely*
  - *Sometimes*

  >*I **often have to** work at the weekend to get everything done.*

  With other **modal verbs.** 

  >*Motorists **will have to** wait until next year to use the bridge*  

- **Have got to (informal)**   
  If have is **contracted** (I've, He's, It'd)

  >*I need a new pair of shoes, and **they've got to** be blue.* 

### Negative sentences (not have to)
- **Not have to**  
  When **somebody else** or **external rules** make something unnecessary

  > *We've been told that we **don't have to** be at work until ten tomorrow*

## Internal necessity 
### Personal necessity (need to/need)
- **Affirmative sentences (ordinary verb)**
  Used in **informal language**

  > *I **needed to** leave early*

  > *She's thirsty. She **needs** a drink*

- **Negative sentences (modal)**  
  Often used in **formal language**. When it is the speaker who decides the lack of necessity 

  Often used with:
  - *Apply*
  - *Concern*
  - *Fear*
  - *Involve*
  - *Mean*
  - *Panic*

  > *I was very nervous before the interview, but I **needn't** have to worried - I got the job!*

### General necessity (don't need to)
- >*You **don't need to** be over 18 to get into a nightclub.*  

## Recommendations
### General comments (should/ought to)
- **Should (common)**  

  >*You **should** finish your homework before going out.*

  Giving advice **with "I"**

  > ***I should** leave early tomorrow*

  **Outside authority** recommands 

  >*The manual says that the computer **should** be disconnected from the power supply before the cover is removed.*


- **Ought to (formal)**

  >*You **ought to** finish you homework before you go out.*

### Present, specific comments (had better)
- **Had better (informal)**  
  To express particular **urgency or in demands and threats**

  >*If you're not well, **you'd better** ask Clare to go instead*

# Conclusion 
## Past events (must have)
- **Must + have + (past participle)**  

  >*That's not Clara's car. She **must have borrowed** it from her parents*

## Happening events (must be/have (got) to be)
- **Must be + (-ing)**  
  Something happening at or around the time of speaking.  

  > *I can't hear a noise. You **must be imagining** things.*  

- **Must be + (bare infinitive) / Have (got) to be**  
  Informal present situation

  > *Their goalkeeper **has got to be** at least two metres tall!*  

## Future (must be going/must be)
- **Must be going/must be**
  
  > *-What are the workmen doing?*  
  > *-I think they **must be going** to dig up the road.*  

# Regret & Apology
## Be sorry about something didn't happen (should/ought to have)
- **Should/Ought to have + (past participle)**

  >*We **should/ought to have** waited for the rain to stop*

## Criticise (shouldn't/oughtn't to have)
- **Shouldn't/Oughtn't to have + (past participle)**

  >*We **shouldn't/oughtn't to have** gone to that place*

# Pattern 
## Compensation 
- **May/might not + (bare infinitive) ... but ...**  
  May/might not have + (past participle) ... but ... (past tense).  

  We say that a person or thing **compensates** to some extent for a **limitation** or **weakness** by having **another characteristic**

  >*The painting **may not be** a masterpiece, **but** the colours are remarkable.*
