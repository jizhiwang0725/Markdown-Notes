# Intentions 
## Planned intentions
### Intentional verbs + infinitive
- **Present simple**
  - *Agree to*
  - *Promise to*
  - *Resolve to*
  - *Undertake to*
  
  > *She **resolves to** complete her degree early.*

- **Present continuous**
  Some verbs that can be used to talk about intentions 
  - *Aiming to*
  - *Expecting to*
  - *Hoping to*
  - *Intend to*
  - *Wanting to*
  - *Proposing to*

- **Be looking to**   
  Means **planning** a course of action. Particularly in **speech and in journalism.**  

  > *We're looking to create 3000 jobs in the city over the next year.*

- **Past simple/continuous**   
  Future events seen from the past 

  >*Nathan **had resolved to** become fluent in Spanish before he left university*

### Present continuous
- **Fixed arrangement**  
  You have already taken steps to organize it, such as **setting a date, time, or buying a ticket.**  

  > *I **am flying** to Paris on Friday.*

- **Firm intention**  
  Sometimes indicates that we have a **firm intention** or have made a definite decision to do something, although this may **NOT already be arranged**

  > ***I'm not asking** Tom to the party*

### Be going to/Will
- **Informal**
  > *I am **going to** study tonight.*   

- **Formal**   
  > *The conference **will** start at 8 in the morning.*

## Unfulfilled past intention
### Past tense
- Talk about things that we **intended** to do, but **didn't.**  
  - *Consider*
  - *Expect to*
  - *Hope to*
  - *Think about*
  - *Want to*

  > *I **had hoped/hoped/was hoping/had been hoping** to visit the gallery before I left Florence, but it's closed on Mondays*  

  > *We **meant/were meaning** to call in and see you, but Marc wasn't feeling well.*

### Was/were going to
- Everyday conversation  

  > *I **was going to** call you, but I fell asleep.*

## Decision at moment of speaking 
### Will 
- Usually used as an **offer, request**
  
  > ***I'll** pick him up at eight.*

# Prediction
## Based on opinion or experience 
### Will
- Often used with:
  - *I bet (informal)*
  - *I reckon*
  - *I expect*
  - *I hope*
  - *I imagine*
  - *I wonder*
  - *I'm sure*
  
  > *Do you **reckon he'll** say yes?*

### Be going to
- Can also be used with those phrases, particularly in **informal contexts**
  
  > *I am expecting him to say yes*

## Based on present evidence 
### Be going to
- Prediction is based on **obvious evidences you just saw.** 
 
  > *The sky's gone really dark. **There's going to be** a storm.*

# Expectation
## Certainly to happen
### Very near future
- **Be about to**  
  Often used for daily conversation

  > ***We're about to eat**. Do you want to join us*

- **Be on the verge of + (-ing/noun)**  
  Often describes something negative.  

  > *People are **on the verge of** starvation as the drought continues*
- **Be on the/brink of/point of + (-ing/noun)**  
  Usually refers to something **important, exciting, or very bad.**  
  
  > *Scientists are **on the brink of** making major advances in the fight against AIDS*

### Near future
- **Be sure/bound to**
  > *-Will there be somewhere to get a coffee at the station?*  
  *-Oh, yes, there **is bound to** be.*

### Particular time 
- **Be due to**  
  > *The company's chief executive **is due to** retire next week*

## Ready to happen 
- **Be set to**
  > *Her new film **is set to** be a great success*

# Viewing From the Past
## Events happened
### Past tense 
- Use the **past tense** of the expressions above.  

  > *The exam was so easy that most people **would have finished** after 30 minutes.*

### Was/were + (infinitive)
- > *At the time she was probably the past actor in the theatre company, but in fat some of her colleagues **were to become** much better known*

## Events expected but didn't happen
### Was/were to have + (past participle)
- **Formal** 

  > *The boat, which **was to have taken** them to the island, failed to arrive.*

### Be supposed to
- **Informal**
  
  > *I **was supposed to** help, but I was ill.*

## Events still in the future
### Present/Past tense  
- The future **seen from the past** is **still in the future** for the speaker.  

  > *It was announced this morning that there **is/was to be** a statement this evening.*
