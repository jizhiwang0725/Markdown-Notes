# Independent Prepositions
## Time
| Preposition | Core meaning | Use case | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **In** | Broad periods | Months, years, decades, seasons. | *- I was originally born **in** the year 1999.*<br> *- We usually travel abroad **in** the summer months.* |
| **During** | Broad periods | Short events or continuous time. | *- The President made the speech **during** his visit.*<br> *- She sneezed loudly **during** the long musical performance.* |
| **On** | Targeted days | Dates, days, holidays with day. | *- Let's plan to meet up **on** Monday afternoon.*<br> *- My birthday is exactly **on** October the 5th.* |
| **At** | Precise moments | Clock times, mealtimes, holiday periods. | *- The new action movie starts exactly **at** 8:00 PM.*<br> *- Many extended families gather together **at** Christmas time.* |
| **Over** | Continuous time | Time that goes on for a length. | *- Weather conditions have been improving **over** recent days.* |
| **Until / Till** | Ending time | Continued to a particular specific time. | *- We have to be at home **until** 2:30.* |
| **By** | Deadline | No later than a highly specific time. | *- Please submit the final report **by** Friday afternoon.* |
| **For** | Duration | Indicates exactly how long an event lasts. | *- I have studied grammar rules **for** three hours.* |
| **From** | Starting point | Exact beginning of a time range. | *- The local coffee shop is open **from** nine.* |
| **Upon** | Immediate sequence | Happening exactly at that exact moment. | *- **Upon** arriving home, she immediately locked the door.* |
| **Within** | Time limit | Happening before a specific period ends. | *- You must reply to us **within** three days.* |
| **Throughout**| Entire time | Occurring from the beginning to end. | *- The festive music played **throughout** the long night.* |
| **Prior to** | Time sequence | Happening before a specific time period. | *- Please submit the official documents **prior to** deadlines.* |

## Physical space

| Preposition | Core meaning | Use case | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **In** | Large areas | Enclosed spaces, cities, major countries. | *- The heavy car keys are **in** my pocket.*<br> *- She currently lives and works **in** downtown Tokyo.* |
| **On** | Physical surfaces | Streets, avenues, large public transport. | *- Please put the hot plate **on** the table.*<br> *- I am currently riding **on** a speeding train.* |
| **At** | Precise spots | Exact addresses, specific points, landmarks. | *- He is currently standing **at** the front entrance.*<br> *- I am patiently waiting **at** the bus stop.* |
| **Across** | Opposite side | The other side of something wide. | *- Antonio lives in the house **across** the road.* |
| **Over** | Vertical level | Directly over or covering something. | *- **Over** the door was a sign for safety.* |
| **Under** | Vertical level | Directly below or entirely covered by. | *- The missing keys were hidden **under** the table.* |
| **Above** | Vertical level | Higher level, not directly positioned over. | *- They lived in a village **above** the lake.* |
| **Below** | Vertical level | Lower level, not directly positioned under. | *- The sunken ship rests **below** the ocean surface.* |
| **By** | Proximity | Physical location positioned right next to something. | *- He sat quietly **by** the large glass window.* |
| **Upon** | Formal placement | Acts as formal replacement for on. | *- He placed the heavy gold crown **upon** his head.* |
| **Within** | Physical boundary | Safely inside a strict physical limit. | *- Please stay safely **within** the designated parking area.* |
| **Throughout**| Entire space | Occurring in every part of place. | *- The new rules apply **throughout** the entire building.* |
| **In front of**| Spatial position | Location directly ahead of physical something. | *- He parked his car right **in front of** mine.* |

## Movement

| Preposition | Core meaning | Use case | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Across** | Horizontal movement | Reaching the other side of something. | *- He quickly jumped right **across** the rushing stream.* |
| **Over** | Vertical movement | Reaching the other side of high. | *- He bravely jumped **over** the fence into gardens.* |
| **Along** | Linear movement | Following a specific line of something. | *- They walked **along** the footpath to the bridge.* |
| **Through** | In and out | Movement in a three-dimensional space. | *- She walked **through** the forest to her house.* |
| **To** | Destination | Physical movement toward a highly specific place. | *- She walked very quickly **to** the grocery store.* |
| **From** | Origin | Where something or someone originally started from. | *- He originally comes all the way **from** Brazil.* |
| **Into** | Movement inside | Moving toward the inside of something. | *- She walked quickly **into** the large dark room.* |
| **Onto** | Surface movement | Moving directly to top of surface. | *- The small cat jumped **onto** the kitchen counter.* |

## Causation and motivation

| Preposition | Core meaning | Use case | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **For** | Purpose | Indicates the specific reason something is done. | *- I happily bought this special gift **for** you.* |
| **Because of**| Cause and reason | Direct reason something has happened. | *- The outdoor concert was canceled **because of** rain.* |
| **Out of** | Origin or motive | Internal reason driving an action. | *- She asked the detailed question **out of** curiosity.* |

## Method and agency
| Preposition | Core meaning | Use case | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **By** | Method or means | How exactly something is done. | *- We successfully travelled to the beautiful city **by** car.* |
| **By** | Agency | Indicates exactly who performed a specific action. | *- The highly detailed report was written **by** him.* |
| **Without** | Absence | Indicates complete lack of a specific thing. | *- He immediately left the house **without** his keys.* |
| **Without** | Condition | Something cannot happen if another thing fails. | *- She managed to survive **without** any outside help.* |

## Abstract relationships
| Preposition | Core meaning | Use case | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **To** | Recipient | Indicates who is receiving an action directly. | *- Give the official documents **to** the regional manager.* |
| **Into** | Transformation | Indicates distinct change in a physical state. | *- The caterpillar magically turned **into** a beautiful butterfly.* |
| **Onto** | Awareness | Knowing about a secret or hidden plot. | *- The clever detective was finally **onto** their scheme.* |
| **On behalf of**| Representation | Speaking as a representative for someone else. | *- I am officially speaking **on behalf of** clients.* |
| **Instead of** | Substitution | Choosing one specific option over another alternative. | *- I will drink hot tea **instead of** coffee.* |
| **According to**| Reference | Specific source of information or a rule. | *- The project is proceeding exactly **according to** plan.* |
| **Out of** | Depletion | No longer having supply of a resource. | *- The local grocery store is completely **out of** milk.* |


# Independent Pairs
## Between & Among/Amongst

| Preposition | Rules | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Between** | - Use between for distinct, individual separate items<br> - It is used for comparison and relationships<br> - Choices and discussions | - *The trade negotiations **between** Brazil, Argentina, and Chile* |
| **Among/Amongst** | - Use among for items in an undivided mass group | - *The big secret spread quickly **among** the large crowd* |
| **Either** | - Something **divided** or **shared** between people | *- The money is to be divided **between/among** the towns in the area* |

## Beside & Besides

| Preposition | Rules | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Beside** | - Use beside to indicate a physical location next to | - *Please pull up a comfortable chair **beside** me* |
| **Besides** | - Use besides to mean in addition to someone | - *Who is going to the party **besides** you?* |


# Dependent Prepositions
## Origin
- Points back to the starting point, source material, or acts as a barrier

### Of (content & origin)
| Category | Trigger words | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Adjectives** | Afraid, Aware, Fond,<br> Sure, Tired, Worthy | *- He threw a party **worthy of** a millionaire.* |
| **Nouns** | Cause, Advantage, Disadvantage,<br> Lack, Understanding, Example,<br> Proof, Habit | *- The main **advantage of** this framework is speed.* |
| **Intransitive prep. verbs** | Approve, Hear, Know,<br> Speak, Talk, Listen | *- Have you ever actually **heard of** anyone*<br> *getting arrested for gossiping in here before?* |
| **Transitive prep. verbs** | Tell, Accuse, Remind,<br> Deprive, Rob, Cure | *- They **accused** the senior project manager **of***<br> *stealing the confidential financial documents last week.* |

### From (separation & barrier)
| Category | Trigger words | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Intransitive prep. verbs** | Recover, Suffer, Stem,<br> Detract, Emerge, Hide | *- The young athlete **recovered from** the injury*<br> *after completing several months of physical therapy.* |
| **Transitive prep. verbs** | Deter, Discourage, Keep,<br> Prevent, Prohibit, Stop | *- The noise from next door **prevented** me*<br> ***from** getting any deep sleep last night.* |

## Direction
- Acts as an arrow pointing toward a benchmark, recipient, or physical target

### To (reactions & transfers)
| Category | Trigger words | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Adjectives** | Similar, Prior, Equivalent,<br> Accustomed, Opposed, Prone,<br> Immune | *- I have always been quite **prone to** headaches.* |
| **Nouns** | Solution, Reaction, Reply,<br> Response, Approach, Threat,<br> Damage, Answer, Alternative | *- They found a **solution to** the memory leak.* |
| **Intransitive prep. verbs** | Adapt, Adjust, Admit,<br> Look forward, Own up,<br> Confess | *- She slowly **confessed to** stealing the missing money.* |
| **Transitive prep. verbs** | Pass, Announce, Explain,<br> Submit, Transfer, Hand | *- She **announced** her final decision **to** the public.* |

### At (specific aim)
| Category | Trigger words | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Intransitive prep. verbs** | Look, Stare, Point,<br> Shout, Smile, Marvel,<br> Guess, Arrive, Stop | *- I turned around and **smiled at** him.* |
| **Transitive prep. verbs** | Aim, Point, Throw,<br> Direct, Fire, Toss | *- He **aimed** the pointer exactly **at** the screen.* |

## Purpose
- Answers "Why?" or "For Whom?"

### For (purpose & beneficiary)
| Category | Trigger words | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Adjectives** | Ready, Eligible, Fit,<br> Notorious, Famous, Responsible | *- The company is **notorious for** paying bills late.* |
| **Nouns** | Need, Demand, Reason,<br> Preference, Reputation, Responsibility | *- There is high **demand for** efficient sorting algorithms.* |
| **Intransitive prep. verbs** | Wait, Apologize, Apply,<br> Search, Look, Ask | *- The angry customer **waited for** the store manager.* |
| **Transitive prep. verbs** | Collect, Mend, Repair,<br> Build, Fix, Buy | *- He quickly **fixed** the broken tap **for** me.* |

## Interaction
- Shows collision, contrast, or reliance between distinct entities

### With (friction & collisions)
| Category | Trigger words | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Adjectives** | Angry, Obsessed, Okay,<br> Satisfied, Familiar, Involved,<br> Associated | *- You should not become too emotionally **involved***<br> ***with** the young children in your medical care.* |
| **Nouns** | Relationship, Connection, Difficulty,<br> Trouble, Sympathy | *- He had no **difficulty with** the Python syntax.* |
| **Intransitive prep. verbs** | Agree, Sympathize, Argue,<br> Fight, Collide, Connect | *- I strongly **agree with** your recent technical assessment.* |
| **Transitive prep. verbs** | Connect, Associate, Supply,<br> Provide, Equip, Entrust | *- The university **equips** the new engineering students **with***<br> *the latest software tools for the Java project.* |

### On (dependency & impact)
| Category | Trigger words | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nouns** | Effect, Impact, Influence,<br> Attack, Dependence, Reliance | *- The update had a negative **impact on***<br> *the internal server's overall daily execution performance.* |
| **Intransitive prep. verbs** | Focus, Concentrate, Deliberate,<br> Rest, Insist, Intrude,<br> Depend, Rely | *- Newspaper editors are being urged not to **intrude***<br> ***on** the grief of the missing servicemen's families.* |
| **Transitive prep. verbs** | Base, Blame, Congratulate,<br> Impose, Inflict, Spend | *- They **based** the new movie **on** a true story.* |

### Between (distinctions)
| Category | Trigger words | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nouns** | Difference, Comparison, Connection,<br> Relationship, Contrast | *- The **difference between** the two variables is negligible.* |

## State
- Highlights immersion, the specific tool used, or a functional capacity

### In (immersion & state)
| Category | Trigger words | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nouns** | Increase, Decrease, Rise,<br> Fall, Interest, Experience,<br> Belief, Delay | *- We saw a massive **increase in** network traffic.* |
| **Intransitive prep. verbs** | Believe, Trust, Confide,<br> Participate, Engage, Result,<br> Culminate, Major | *- Their many years of research have finally **culminated***<br> ***in** a successful cure for the viral disease.* |
| **Transitive prep. verbs** | Involve, Invest, Immerse,<br> Include, Entangle, Implicate | *- Do not **involve** me **in** your petty argument.* |

### As (roles)
| Category | Trigger words | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Intransitive prep. verbs** | Work, Speak, Serve,<br> Act, Qualify, Double | *- I currently **work as** a senior software engineer.* |
| **Transitive prep. verbs** | Use, Describe, Regard,<br> See, Treat, Classify | *- Please **use** this rolled-up towel **as** a pillow.* |

### By (mechanisms)
| Category | Trigger words | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Intransitive prep. verbs** | Begin, Close, End,<br> Finish, Open, Start | *- Can you **begin by** cleaning the kitchen floor?* |
| **Transitive prep. verbs** | Multiply, Divide, Judge,<br> Measure, Evaluate, Assess | *- You must **multiply** the first variable **by** ten.* |
