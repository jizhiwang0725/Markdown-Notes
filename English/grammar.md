# Types of Clauses 
## Time clause
- Begin with a specific **time-related word.**  
- For example words like:
  - *When*
  - *While*
  - *Before*
  - *Since*
  - *By the time*
  - *Until*

# Verbs 
## State verbs
### Definition 
- Describes a state of being, **a condition, a feeling, or a belief**, rather than a physical action.

## Dual meaning verbs
- Some verbs can act as **either a state verb or an action verb**, but their meaning changes depending on how they are used 

# (Tenses) Present Simple and Present Continuous
## Present simple
### Describing physical states 
- Verbs describing **possession and location.** 
  Such as: 
  - *Consist of*  
  - *Own* 

  *The app doesn't **appear** to work on my phone.*  
  (Meaning "seem.")

### Describing mental states
- Verbs describing **preferences and mental states.**   
  Words like:
  - *Agree*
  - *Believe*
  - *Conclude*
  - *Know*
  - *Prefer*
  - *Doubt*

  *I **believe** you now.* 

### Performative verbs (Affirmative)
- The act of **saying the word is the action itself.**  
  Words like 
  - *Agree*
  - *Congratulate* 
  - *Permit* 
  - *Refuse* 
  
  *I **agree** with your plan.*  
  (Saying the word "agree" is the act of agreeing)

### Refer to the contents
- Use the present simple to refer to the **contents** of books, films, newspapers, etc. 
 
  *Thompson **gives** a list of the largest European companies in Chapter 6.*

### Highlight an event 
- Often used **after past tense** and a phrase such as **suddenly or all of a sudden**
  
  *I was sitting in the part, reading a newspaper, when **all of a sudden** this dog **jumps** at me* 

## Present continuous  
### Describing a temporary situation 
- Emphasise that a situation is **temporary** or for a period of **time around the present**
  
  *Ella stays with us quite often. The children **are loving** having her around.*  
  (A time a round the present) 

### Describing mental states
- To emphasise that we have recently **started to think about something** or that we are **not sure about something**

    *I'm **regretting** my decision to give her the job.*  
    (I am **increasingly aware** that it was the wrong decision)

### Habits 
- **Describe something we regularly do at a certain time**  
  Can be used with frequency adverbs such as: 
  - *Always*
  - *Constantly* 
  - *Continually* 
  - *Forever*
  
  *At eight o'clock I'm **usually driving** to work, so phone me on my mobile.*

## Present tense
### Describing an action 
- Verbs can take either the **simple or continuous form**, depending on the context.  

  *Carley Robb is currently **appearing** in a musical on Broadway.*  
  (Meaning "to take part.")

### Performative verbs (negative)
- **The negation of performative verbs**  
  Can take either cases.  
  
  ***I don't deny/I'm not denying** taking the books, but Miguel said it would be okay*

### Telling stories
- **Informal stories and jokes**  
  To create the impression that events are happening now.  
  
  *She **goes** up to this man and **looks** straight into his eyes. He's not **wearing** his glasses, and he **doesn't recognise** her...*  

- **Live commentaries**  
  When the report takes place at the **same time as the action** (for example, on sport events).  

- **Introducing news**  
  To introduce news that we have heard, read, seen, or being told.
  Phrases such as: 
    - *It says here*
    - *I hear/gather/see/understand*
    - *They say, (Someone) says, (Someone) tells me* 

  *I **gather/gathered** you're worried about Pedro.*  

# (Tenses) Past Simple and Present Prefect 
## Past Simple
### Describing events
- **Refers to past, completed events**.

  *After she **left (past)** hospital, she **had** a long holiday*

  *The minute I **got (past)** the news about Anna I **telephoned** my parents*

## Present Perfect
### Describing events
- **Refers to future events.**
  
  *After Lucas **has left (future)** school, he **will** be spending six months in India.*

  *I **will** contact you the minute I **have got (future)** my exam result*

### First time of experiencing  
- After the pattern:
  - *It/This/That is/will be the first time* 
  
  *That's the first time **I've seen** Jan look embarrassed*  
  (Reporting a past event)

## Either
### With word "Since"
- **Past simple** verb in the **time clause**, and a **present perfect** verb in the **main clause**, and a 
  
  *Since Mr Dodson **became** president unemployment **has increased**.*

- When two situations in the main clause and time clause **extend until the present**, use **present perfect** for both.  
  
  ***Have** you **met** any of your neighbours since you **have lived** here?*

### News reports 
- **Present perfect** is used to **introduce** a recent event, and then the **past simple or other past tenses** are used to **give details**.   
  
  *A Russian spacecraft **has returned** safely to Earth with its two passengers. US astronaut Scott Keane and Russian cosmonaut Olga Kaleri **landed** in the early hours of Wednesday.*

# (Tenses) Past Simple and Past Continuous
## Past simple
### Describing actions
- Usually used for **repeated past actions**.
  
  *We **went** to Spain three times last year.*

## Past continuous
### Describing actions
- To emphasise that repeated actions went on for a **limited and temporary period** of time.  
  Usually for **spoken English**

  *When Kata was in hospital, we **visited/were visiting** her twice a day*

- Talk about something that happened **surprisingly often** 
  
  *Last week I **was having** to bring work home every night to get it all done.* 

### Intend to do in the future
- **Can be used to report what we might do in the future.**  
  Is **less definite** than **present continuous**

  Words for example:
  - *Thinking of*
  - *Wondering about*
  - *Want to*
  
  *I **was thinking of** going to China next year, but it depends on how much money we've got.*  
  (**Less definite** than "I'm thinking of")

## Either
### Intention
- **To talk about things people had intended but didn't manage to do.**   
  Usually followed by a conjunction that gives a **contrast**.  

  Words for example:  
  - *Consider*
  - *Expect to*
  - *Hope to*
  - *Think about*
  - *Want to*
    
  *We **meant/were meaning** to call in and see you, but Marc wasn't feeling well.*

# (Tenses) Past Simple and Past Perfect 
## Order of past event 
- **Events using past simple are events happened before past.**  
  When we **understand** that we are talking about **events before another past event**, we don't have to continue using the past perfect 
  
  *We **bought** a new car last month. **We'd driven** my parents' old car for ages, but it **started/had started** to fall apart. Furthermore, we **put/had put** a new engine in it, but that **didn't solve/hadn't solved** the problems we were having.*

- If the **order of past events** is clear from the context, we can often use either 
  
  ***After** Ivan **had finished/finished** reading, he put out the light*

## Past perfect
### Reporting
- Often used in reporting what was originally **said or thought** in the **present perfect or past simple**
  
  *I was sure that I **had met** him before.*  
  (I said 'I **have met** him before.')

  *Police said that 225 people **had drowned/drowned** in the recent flood.*  
  (The policed said that previously.)

## Either
### Intentions
- Talk about things that we **intended** to do, but **didn't or won't now do in the future.**  

  *I **had hoped/hoped/was hoping/had been hoping** to visit the gallery before I left Florence, but it's closed on Mondays*

# (Tenses) Present Perfect and Present Perfect Continuous
## Present perfect continuous
### Finished repeated activities with perceivable results
- Something that has recently finished and the result of it is still present.  
  Normally for extended or repeated activity
  
  ***I've been playing** squash and need a shower!*

### Ongoing
- To express the idea of an activity (a task, piece of work, etc.) in progress **until recently or until the time of speaking**.  
  **Time expressions** are usually used to say **how long the activity** has been in progress.  

  ***Have you been working** in the garden **all day**? You look exhausted.*

### Ongoing and repeated
- To emphasise that an activity is **ongoing and repeated.** 
  
  *Miguel **has been kicking** a football against the wall all day*

### Draw a conclusion 
- To draw a conclusion from **what we can see, hear, etc**.  
  Often use to **complain or criticise**.  

  *Who **has been messing** around with my papers? They're all over the place.*

## Present perfect
### Finished single event with perceivable results
- Not repeated, but the result/consequence is still obvious
  
  *He **has broken** his finger and is in a lot of pain*

  *Price **have decreased** by 7%*

### Unchanging state
- Verbs that describes an **unchanging state**  
  Such as: 
  - *Belong*
  - *Know*
  - *(Dis)like*
  - *Understand*
  
  ***Have you known** each other long?* 

### Number of occasions
- Activity happened **only once or on a specific number of occasions**. 

  *He **has played** for the national team in 65 matches so far*  

## Either
### General circumstances
- When we talk about situations (**general characteristics or circumstances**) that exist until the present.  
  
  ***We've been looking/We've looked** forward to this holiday for ages*

# (Tenses) Past Perfect Continuous, Past Perfect and Past Continuous 
## Past Perfect continuous 
### Describing an ongoing past event 
- Usually comes with time expressions.  
- **Finished at that time**

  ***We'd been driving** for about **an hour** when the engine suddenly stopped*  
  
- **Continued beyond it**
  
  *She felt terrible during the interview because she **had been suffering** from flu **since** the previous day*

- **Finished shortly before** 
  
  *When I last saw Omar, **he'd been running** and was out of breath*

### Describing states 
- *We **had only owned** the car **for six weeks** when the clutch broke* 

### Written text 
- **Occurs more often in written texts rather than daily speeches** 
  
  *The body of a climber who went missing in the Alps was finally found yesterday. Carl Sims **had been climbing** along near the Harz Waterfall, which has claimed many lives in the past.*

## Past perfect 
### Repeated events 
- How **many times** something happened in a period up to a particular pastime.  
  
  *I **had stayed** in the hotel **twice** in the 1990s.*  

## Past continuous 
### Ongoing past event 
- Usually without a time expression because we don't care about the duration
  
  *Karin met Lars when she **was going out** with his best friend.*
