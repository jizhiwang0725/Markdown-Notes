Spelling Rules

The 7-letter spelling rule

After the letters к, г, х, ш, щ, ж and ч, do not write the letter ы. Write и instead.

The 5-letter spelling rule

After the letters ш, щ, ж, ч and ц, do NOT write unstressed о; write е instead.

The 8-letter spelling rule

After the letters к, г, х, ж, ч, ш, щ and ц change ю to у and я to а

Especially for И-conjugation verbs

(Cases) Nominative case

The base form of the word

When to use (subject)

  1. The grammatical subject of the sentence
    The noun or pronoun that "does" the verb:
    Джон американец - John is an American

  2. Stating identity (X is Y)
    The word or phrase that comes after forms of the verb to be: is, are, was, were:
    Джон американец - John is an American

  3. Naming and listing things

    • Street signs
    • Store names
    • Items on a shopping list
  • the title of a book or movie

(Cases) Genitive case

When to use (possession)

Possession ("of" or "s") and after preposition для
Телефон брата.
(The phone of my brother.)

Absence
The word "нет" always forces the noun that follows it into the genitive case

У меня нет проблемы. (I do not have a problem)

Saying "I have" (У + genitive)
By/At [person], there is [object].
The proposition У (by/at) always requires the genitive case.

У меня ...
(I have ...)
У сестры есть ...
(The sister has ...)

Quantities and numbers
When ever specify how much or how many of something there is, the noun being counted drops into the genitive case. This applies to words like много (a lot) and мало (a little), as well as numbers.

Много воды.
(A lot of water.)

Стакан сока.
(A bottle of water)

After prepositions

English Russian
From (out of) Из
From (a person/point) От
After После
Near/Around Около

(Cases) Accusative case

When to use (object)

Object
Identify the direct object of a sentence - what is receiving the action (comes after a verb).

Я люблю русскую литературу.
Я изучала страноведение России.

Destination and motion
When you are moving toward a place or placing an object into a location, you use the preposition "в" or "на". This answers the question Куда?

Я иду в университет.
(I am going to the university.)
Мы едем в ресторан.
(We are going to a restaurant.)

Specific time expressions
When an event happens, and expression for duration of time.

В пятницу.
(On Friday.)
Я работал весь день.
(I worked all day)

When to use (instrument)

The tool or instrument (with something)

Accompaniment (together with). After preposition "с"

When talking about someone's profession using certain verbs, the profession goes into the instrumental case.

(Pronouns) What - Что vs. Какой

Russian uses two different words for what

Что (answer in noun)

  • The answer is a noun
  • Never means which or which one
  • Что never combines with another noun: что чемодан?

Examples
Что это? (What is that?)
Что в чемодане? (What's in the case?)

Какой (answer in adjective)

Masculine Neuter Feminine Plural
Какой Какое Какая Какие
  • The answer is usually an adjective
  • Means which or which one
  • Какой combines with other nouns: Какой чемодан?

Examples
Какая книга! (What a book!)
Какое место? (What(which) place?)

(Pronouns) “This is” vs. "This": это vs. этот (эта, это, эти)

The ending word in a sentence is emphasized.

Introductory это (this is/those are)

The object is emphasized.

Это + Adjective/Possessive pronoun + Noun

  • Begins full sentences (This is a...)
  • Indeclinable
  • Means:
    • this is
    • that is
    • these are
    • those are

Examples
Это небольшой словарь. (This is a small dictionary.)
Это маленькое окно. (This is a small window.)
Это интересная фотография. (This is an interesting photo.)
Это красивые дома. (Those are pretty houses.)

Declinable этот

The appearance is emphasized

Этот/Это/Эта/Эти + Noun + Adjective/Possessive pronoun

Masculine Neuter Feminine Plural
этот это эта эти
  • Does not create full sentences
  • Works like modifiers мой, твой, наш, ваш
  • Means:
    • this
    • these or that
    • those

Examples
Этот словарь небольшой. (This dictionary is small.)
Это окно маленькое. (This window is small.)
Эта фотография интересная. (This photo is interesting.)
Эти дома красивые. (These houses are beautiful.)

(Adjective) Short Adjectives

Definition

Short adjectives are adjectives without their endings. They act as a predicate to describe a state.

They do NOT follow any cases, but matches the gender and number of the noun they describe.

Endings

Fill vowel quirk (Masculine)
When you chop the ending of a long adjective, you are sometimes left over with two consonants smashed together at the end of the word, making it impossible to pronounce.

To resolve this, Russian inserts a "fill vowel" (usually е or о) into the masculine form only to make it roll off the tongue.

(Verbs) Reflexive verbs

Definition

Reflexive Non-reflexive
The doer and the receiver are the same The doer and the receiver are NOT the same

The sentence is "completed" at the reflexive verb, any phrases after that are just additional information about the action performed.

Я учусь в университете (additional information)

Endings (-ся/-сь)

Regular conjugations + ending

Consonants Vowels

  1. When used with "мы", it often refers to a reciprocal action (doing something to each other)

    Мы встречаемся.

If the verb itself isn't reflexive, but you want to express that "we" are doing something to "ourselves", or that "someone" is doing something to "oneself".

(Verbs) Imperfective and perfective verbs

Imperfective Perfective
Ongoing processes Completed processes
Habit One-time
- Sequence of completed process

Imperfective verbs

The action is on going and is a process
You started the action, but not finished.
Я читал книгу.
(I was reading a book.)

Habit
Я обычно покупаю кофе.
(I usually buy coffee.)

Perfective verbs

The action is completed with a result
You started the action, finished it, and there is a result to show for it.

Я прочитал книгу.
(I finished the book)

A one-time, specific action
The action happened once at a specific moment, rather than being a habit or a routine

Сегодня я купил чай.
(Today, I bought tea)

A sequence of completed actions (step-by-step)
If you are telling a story where one action finishes and then the next one starts.

Он встал, одлся и вышел.
(He got up, got dressed, and left)

⚠️ Perfective verbs do NOT have PRESENT tense
When take a perfective verb and conjugate it using standard present-tense endings, it automatically creates the future perfective tense.

Познакомитесь!
(Get acquainted!)

Recognize perfective verbs from imperfective verbs

Adding a prefix
Add short prefix (like по-, с-, про-, на-) to the imperfective verb.
делать (to do) - сделать (to get done)

Changing a suffix / internal letter
покупать (to be buying) - ()купить (to buy)

Completely different words
говорить (to speak/say) - сказать (to say/tell)

(Tenses) Present Tense

"To be" in present tense

The present tense of "to be" (am, is, are) is absent in Russian:

Я студент - I am a student
Я студентка - I am a student

In writing, a dash is sometimes used in place of the verb "to be" when both the subject and the predicate are nouns:

Москва - русский город.
